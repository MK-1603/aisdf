# Source Quality and Verification

Prefer, in order: official specifications/documentation and standards; source code/release notes/security advisories; peer-reviewed research; recognized technical institutions; reputable technical reporting; community discussions as discovery leads. Verify critical claims against primary sources. Record conflicts, publication dates, scope and limitations. Community posts are useful signals, not authoritative proof of compatibility or security.

## Additional detail (carried from the starter seed)
Evaluate sources for authority, ownership, recency, specificity, reproducibility, version match, bias and applicability.

For consequential claims:
- Prefer primary official sources.
- Inspect the source rather than relying on search snippets.
- Confirm the claim applies to the selected product/version.
- Cross-check independent evidence when appropriate.
- Record contradictions and uncertainty.
- Preserve exact source/version/date.

Community posts can reveal practical problems but should not be sole authority for security, licensing, pricing or compatibility.

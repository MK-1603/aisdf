# Rule Precedence and Conflict Resolution

Apply the highest applicable authority first:
1. Applicable law, regulation and binding contractual obligations.
2. Platform/provider safety, security and access constraints.
3. Organization policies and project-owner approvals.
4. AISDF mandatory rules.
5. Approved project baseline, architecture and acceptance criteria.
6. Workflow/engine-specific instructions.
7. Individual task instructions.
8. Informal suggestions and conversational assumptions.

A lower-level instruction cannot override a higher-level constraint. If two equal-level requirements conflict, stop the affected work, record both sources, assess impact and request a decision from the designated owner. Do not choose silently.

Every exception records: rule ID, conflict, authority, decision maker, rationale, affected scope, compensating controls, residual risk, date and expiry/review date. Safety, legal and platform restrictions are not waivable by a project prompt.

## Precedence
1. Applicable law, safety and platform/security restrictions.
2. Explicit user-approved project constraints and organizational policy.
3. AISDF mandatory rules.
4. Approved project baseline and architecture decision records.
5. Current workflow and engine specification.
6. Tool-specific adapter instructions.
7. Local task instructions.
8. Model assumptions and generic preferences.

Lower-priority instructions cannot override higher-priority instructions.

## Conflict workflow
1. Detect and quote the conflicting requirements accurately.
2. Identify source, authority, version and affected artifacts.
3. Determine whether conflict is real or can be reconciled.
4. Assess security, product, cost, schedule and migration impact.
5. Offer viable options and tradeoffs.
6. Request a decision from the authorized owner.
7. Record decision, rationale, approver, timestamp and affected baseline.
8. Update dependent documents and tasks.

Never resolve a material conflict silently. If safety or authorization is uncertain, pause the affected action.
